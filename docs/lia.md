# PRJGENUI Workforce — Legitimate Interests Assessment (LIA)

**Version:** 1.0 draft for review with Marizilena Silva Fortes · 20 October 2026
**Status:** to be completed and signed off before any real personal data is
processed (commitment approved 20 October 2026; production gate item)

---

## 1. Purpose test

**The processing assessed:** performance and reliability evidence
(scores, bands, per-factor breakdowns, weights versions) built from
assignment history (attendance, ratings, rehire decisions, client and
supervisor feedback), and its use in **re-matching workers to future
requests** (GDPR schedule class 5).

**Why:** to put forward the workers who best fit a request, honestly and
consistently — the core service. Without it, matching would run on
unverifiable CV claims, which is worse for workers with good verified
records and worse for sites that need reliable workers.

**Benefits:** to workers (verifiable history travels with them; new
workers are "insufficient verified history", never poor performers), to
PRJGENUI (matching quality), to contractors (reliable, compliant workers).

**Alternative, less intrusive ways:** ask workers each time (slower, and
the platform would still need assignment history to be useful); use no
history (defeats the service); consent (not appropriate — the processing
is intrinsic to the service and not genuinely optional, so consent would
not be freely given).

## 2. Necessity test

The evidence is **necessary** for matching quality: qualification match
alone cannot distinguish a worker who reliably attends from one who does
not, and attendance/rehire history is the only data that can. The
processing is limited to assignment-connected facts (dates, attendance,
ratings, rehire decisions, feedback), with the per-factor breakdown and
weights version recorded so any score can be explained and reproduced.

## 3. Balance test — impact on workers

**Could workers reasonably expect it?** Yes — the privacy notice
(section 5) explains the matching and scoring in plain language, with the
principal factors named, at first sign-in, before any data is used; the
worker sees their own per-factor breakdown in their passport.

**Would it cause harm or intrusion?** The factors are factual and
assignment-connected; no speculative or discriminatory wording is allowed
in notes (manual data-governance rule). Intrusion is limited by: data
minimisation (only assignment-connected facts), restricted access (own
record; never shown to other workers; not shown to contractors —
contractors see compliance status and match reasons, not internal
scores), retention (relationship end + 6 years, reviewed before
deletion), and audit.

**Can they object and challenge?** Yes — the right to object to
legitimate-interests processing is signposted separately in the privacy
notice (section 6); on objection the processing stops unless a
compelling reason is shown and the worker is told either way. Any record
(rating, attendance, score, readiness decision) can be challenged;
challenges are reviewed by a named person and audited, without erasing the
original (dispute/supersede, never delete).

**Safeguards that make the balance work:**

1. **No solely-automated decisions** — every selection and deployment
   decision is reviewed and confirmed by a named person (enforced by the
   platform: an offer cannot even be created without a recorded approved
   match).
2. **Compliance precedence** — performance evidence can never override a
   failed Right to Work, mandatory card, training or site requirement
   (enforced, migration 0019, verified).
3. **Explainability** — every eligible, hold, review or blocked outcome
   shows the rule and evidence that produced it; scores show their
   factors and weights version.
4. **New workers are never penalised** — "insufficient verified history"
   is not a poor score; confidence grows with records.
5. **The 70-point threshold is a human-review trigger only** — never a
   compliance pass/fail (protected rule).

## 4. Outcome

The legitimate interests of PRJGENUI (honest, quality matching) are
balanced against workers' rights by the safeguards above, which are built
into the platform rather than promised in policy alone. This LIA is
provisional like the rest of the schedule, pending the employment-law
opinion and the DPIA, and is re-reviewed on any material change (new
factors, new automated decision, international expansion, or the
temporary labour supply model).
